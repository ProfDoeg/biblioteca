# Biblioteca: annotated bibliography

**artaud1976selected**  
Artaud, Antonin. *Selected Writings*. Edited by Susan Sontag. Translated by Helen Weaver. New York: Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 1976. University of California Press reprint 1988, ISBN 0520064437.

Sontag's selection across Artaud's whole life: the correspondence with Rivière, the Surrealist texts, The Theatre and Its Double, the Mexican and Tarahumara writings, the Rodez letters and the late radio work, with her long introduction on Artaud as the exemplary case of the modern writer who fails at making a work and succeeds at making a life into one.

File: `books/artaud_1976_selected_writings.pdf`. Source: dokumen.pub scan, sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**attisani2003franciscan**  
Attisani, Antonio and House, Jane. "Franciscan Performance: A Theatre Lost and Found Again." *PAJ: A Journal of Performance and Art* 25, no. 1 (2003): 48–60.

Attisani (translated by House) reads the early Franciscans as a performance practice: Francis as joculator Domini, the preaching in the piazza, the Greccio crib as staged Nativity, the body as the text, and argues that a theatre lost with the order's institutionalization can be recovered as a model for performance now.

File: `articles/attisani_house_2003_franciscan_performance.pdf`. Source: JSTOR, sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**bonaventure1868francis**  
Bonaventure, Saint. *The Life of St. Francis of Assisi (Legenda maior)*. Translated by Elizabeth Lockhart. London: R. Washbourne, 1868. Google Books scan; preface by Henry Edward Manning; Latin original Legenda maior, 1263.

Bonaventure's Legenda maior, the life the order made official in 1266 when it ordered the earlier lives destroyed: Francis's conversion, the rule, the stigmata at La Verna, the death and canonization, arranged by virtues rather than strict chronology. The Victorian translation by Lockhart with Manning's preface, in the same generation that rediscovered Francis for the English.

File: `books/bonaventure_1868_the_life_of_st_francis_of_assisi.pdf`. Source: Google Books scan, sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**dekerckhove1982ecriture**  
de Kerckhove, Derrick. "Écriture, théâtre et neurologie." *Études françaises* 18, no. 1 (1982): 109–128. https://doi.org/10.7202/036754ar.

De Kerckhove's thesis in its 1982 form: the Greek phonetic alphabet, by separating sound from meaning and forcing left-to-right sequential decoding, reorganized the brain's lateralization, and fifth-century tragedy was the public institution that trained an oral population in the new interiority, with the immobilized, speaking Prometheus as the emblematic case. Draws on Havelock, McLuhan, Innis and the split-brain neurology of the 1970s. A private English translation exists in private_notes (not in this repo).

File: `articles/de_kerckhove_1982_ecriture_theatre_et_neurologie.pdf`. Source: érudit open access, fetched by nodus1 2026-09-12 at Anthony's request.

**fideler1993sunofgod**  
Fideler, David. *Jesus Christ, Sun of God: Ancient Cosmology and Early Christian Symbolism*. Wheaton, IL: Quest Books, 1993.

Fideler reads the early Christian symbols (the 888 of Iesous, the 153 fishes, the vesica) as survivals of Pythagorean number cosmology and Greek gematria, with the geometrical constructions drawn out. Used for the 888 video: the hexagon, the cube down its diagonal with eight corners, the Greek gematria table.

File: `books/fideler_1993_jesus_christ_sun_of_god.pdf`. Source: gnosis.study scan, sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-10.

**flaubert1874temptation**  
Flaubert, Gustave. *The Temptation of St. Anthony*. English translation, edition and translator not identified from the scan, 1874. Internet Archive, University of Toronto copy (temptationofstan00flauuoft); French original La Tentation de saint Antoine, 1874.

Flaubert's closet drama of the hermit's night in the desert: the Queen of Sheba, the heresiarchs, the gods passing in procession, Hilarion, the Sphinx and the Chimera, and the last vision of matter. The 1874 version is the third; the scan is an early English translation whose translator the copy does not name.

File: `books/flaubert_1874_the_temptation_of_st_anthony.pdf`. Source: Internet Archive scan, sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**rundin2004pozomoro**  
Rundin, John. "Pozo Moro, Child Sacrifice, and the Greek Legendary Tradition." *Journal of Biblical Literature* 123, no. 3 (2004): 425–447. https://doi.org/10.2307/3268041.

The relief on the Iberian funerary tower of Pozo Moro (c. 500 BCE, near Albacete) shows a two-headed monster on a throne receiving a bowl with a small human figure; Rundin reads it with the Phoenician tophet evidence and the Greek legends of child-devouring (Kronos, Tantalus, Lykaon) as one Mediterranean complex of sacrifice and its mythic memory.

File: `articles/rundin_2004_pozo_moro_child_sacrifice.pdf`. Source: ResearchGate copy, sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**santosgranero2004enemy**  
Santos-Granero, Fernando. "The Enemy Within: Child Sorcery, Revolution, and the Evils of Modernization in Eastern Peru." In *In Darkness and Secrecy: The Anthropology of Assault Sorcery and Witchcraft in Amazonia*, edited by Neil L. Whitehead and Robin Wright. Durham, NC: Duke University Press, 2004.

Among the Ashaninka, Asheninka, Nomatsiguenga and Yanesha of the Selva Central, accusations of child sorcery, thought extinct since the 1970s, returned in the 1990s in the Shining Path years; Santos-Granero reads the child sorcerer as the figure through which a society under revolutionary violence and forced modernization names the enemy inside itself.

File: `articles/santos_granero_2004_the_enemy_within.pdf`. Source: sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**stavrakopoulou2013tophet**  
Stavrakopoulou, Francesca. "The Jerusalem Tophet: Ideological Dispute and Religious Transformation." *Studi Epigrafici e Linguistici* 29--30 (2013): 137–158.

Against the scholarly caricature of the tophet as a foreign Molek cult, Stavrakopoulou argues from the biblical texts themselves that the Ben Hinnom site was a Yahwistic sanctuary of child sacrifice whose later condemnation (Jeremiah, Kings) records an ideological dispute inside Judah's religion, not an import.

File: `articles/stavrakopoulou_2013_the_jerusalem_tophet.pdf`. Source: sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.

**yates1966memory**  
Yates, Frances A.. *The Art of Memory*. London: Routledge and Kegan Paul, 1966. Reprinted as Frances Yates, Selected Works, vol. III, Routledge, London and New York, 1999.

The history of the classical art of memory (places and images, from Simonides and the Ad Herennium through Metrodorus's zodiac) into its medieval, Renaissance and Hermetic transformations: Camillo's Memory Theatre, Lull's wheels, Bruno's Shadows and Seals, Fludd's theatre and the Globe, ending with Leibniz. Read for the symbols repo's memoria catalogue (every system she presents, 176 reader entries merged into dossier-sized systems) and for the zodiac story video's Simonides opening.

File: `books/yates_1966_the_art_of_memory.pdf`. Source: sent by Anthony over Telegram 2026-09-12.
